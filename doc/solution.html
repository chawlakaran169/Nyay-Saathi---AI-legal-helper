Nyay Saathi: an AI legal helper for everyday problems in India

Kuku AI Generalist assignment, Problem 2: Build an AI Lawyer · Karan · 230121033 · Engineering Physics · Code, outputs and demo video linked in the submission form

1. What I built, and why this scope

A chat assistant (text, plus browser voice in English/Hindi) that takes a messy story and helps the person act: it builds the case (what happened, when, how much, what proof), explains where they stand and their options in plain language, drafts a legal notice (English or Hindi), and lets them practise the argument with the assistant playing the other side. It also explains a legal notice the person has received (with a draft reply), gives an evidence checklist and a reminder calendar (.ics), lets them download / print their case file, masks Aadhaar / PAN / phone if they paste them, and has a one-shot mode that turns any pasted story into a full report.

Scope: three problems, in depth, plus received notices: (1) a landlord keeping the security deposit, (2) a refund / defective product or service, (3) unpaid salary. I chose them because they are the brief's own examples, are everyday and money-sized, are decided mostly on documents (so a chat can really help), and each has a clear route (civil court, Consumer Commission, Labour Commissioner). Family, criminal, property-title, tax, cyber and builder/RERA matters are deliberately out of scope: the assistant says so and points to a lawyer / free legal aid (NALSA 15100).

2. Assumptions (the brief was silent)

  • User is an ordinary adult on a phone writing English, Hindi or Hinglish, with a money claim and no lawyer yet. "Help them act" means information + paperwork + rehearsal, not filing; a real lawyer is always recommended before filing.
  • Free tier only. With no API key available, the default build runs offline at zero cost; an LLM is an optional plug-in.
  • Legal content was drafted from my own knowledge, then checked on 8 Nov 2026 against India Code, Gazette notifications and official department sources where available; some practice guidance uses explainers. This is not a lawyer's review. The check found two outdated entries: the Payment of Wages Act and the Industrial Disputes Act were replaced on 21 Nov 2025 by the Code on Wages and the Industrial Relations Code. These were corrected, and each entry stores its verification status. Limits and state rules can change, so answers ask users to confirm them.

4. How it avoids inventing laws, sections and cases

Five layers, from "can't" to "caught": (a) the model never writes legal content (retrieval-free closed list, so there is nothing to confabulate); (b) an output guard scans every message and blocks any Act, Section, case-style citation (X v. Y, AIR, SCC) or new number not in the knowledge base, replacing it with "check with a lawyer"; (c) case-law requests are refused ("AI tools often invent them"); (d) prompt-injection and "guarantee I'll win" are handled; (e) sections are cited only where confidence is high, otherwise just the Act name.

Bugs my tests found (all fixed): keyword "lease" matched inside "please" (substring matching), so every polite message leaned to deposit; the amount parser swallowed "2 months, Rs 70000" (a comma read as a number); the guard wrongly blocked a correct answer because of a long official Rules title; "80000 salary" was parsed as "80000 saal" (years); an unclassifiable message made it repeat one question forever; the first of several amounts was silently taken; the "will I win?" warning was lost when asked in an earlier turn; running my own sample stories showed the date parser used the tenancy start date instead of the move-out date and could not read Hindi months, which would give a false "time-barred" warning.

Where it still fails (honest list; raw in outputs/probes.md):
  • Keyword rules generalise only partly (measured): 98% on messages I tuned against vs 71-80% on fresh ones, 0-4% wrong advice; unplaceable messages get a question, and many out-of-scope topics are "asked" not clearly "referred". Mitigations: the first reply names the detected problem and the user can correct it in one message ("wrong, it's a deposit problem"); amounts/dates can be corrected the same way; an optional LLM fallback (one-word, whitelisted output) is built but has not been separately tested with a live model.
  • Over-caution: "threatened" fires the emergency screen and drops the deposit problem; one out-of-scope word ("harassment") refuses an otherwise in-scope salary claim.
  • Hinglish is understood but answered in English; Tamil/Bengali/etc. are declined unless an LLM is connected.
  • Untested / unreviewed: the owner manually confirmed Punjabi translation and a response using Gemini on their local machine; this is not part of the automated offline test suite. Live topic-classifier fallback, browser voice (mic), and lawyer review remain untested. Selected claims were checked against official India Code, Gazette and department sources. Consumer Commission limits use the value paid as consideration, not necessarily the refund due; state and transition rules may apply. Section 59 of the Industrial Relations Code covers only specified categories of money due to a worker, not every unpaid-salary claim.

6. Scale and cost

  • Cost: offline mode makes 0 model calls (free). With an LLM, English costs 0 calls; Hindi/other-language adds at most 2 small calls per message (translate in, simplify out). I did not measure rupee cost; with a small model it should be a fraction of a rupee per conversation, to be confirmed.
  • New input: python3 run_story.py story.txt (or a folder) reads any story and writes a full report, so a whole batch can be processed with no chat.
  • Adding a legal area = 1 knowledge-base file section + a keyword list + a notice template + 4 roleplay objections + test cases. No retraining. Adding a state's rent act or labour office is a knowledge-base entry.
  • Next steps: lawyer-reviewed knowledge base; semantic classifier (LLM or embeddings) behind the same guard; regional languages; WhatsApp front-end; log (consented) failed conversations to find the next area to cover.

7. AI tools used, and where they got it wrong

Built with AI coding assistance, with me setting scope and reviewing the outputs. Where the AI got it wrong: in testing, the parser read "80000 salary" as 80,000 years; I fixed the date parsing and added a regression check. An early legal draft cited two laws that had been replaced; I updated the knowledge base after checking current sources and recorded the verification limits. Finally, a fake-model probe invented an Act and case; the output guard blocked all 7 attack variants. These failures led to narrower grounded answers, regression tests and an explicit lawyer-review step.
