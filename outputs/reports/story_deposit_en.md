### 2. Where you stand

- A security deposit is your money held by the landlord as safety for unpaid rent or damage beyond normal wear and tear. It should be returned when you hand back the home, after lawful deductions. Read your rental agreement first: it usually states the amount, the notice period and what can be deducted. There is no single national rule on how fast a deposit must be returned; it depends on your agreement and your state's rent law.

- The Model Tenancy Act, 2021 is a model law that each state may adopt. Where a state has adopted it, the deposit for a home is capped at two months' rent and is to be returned when the tenant hands back possession, after lawful deductions. It applies only in states that have adopted it, so check your state's law.

- If the landlord keeps the deposit without a valid reason: (1) ask for it in writing and request itemised deductions; (2) send a legal notice with a deadline, commonly 15 days; (3) if still unpaid, a civil suit for recovery of money can be filed. Consumer forums generally do not hear landlord-tenant deposit disputes; the usual route is the civil court or the rent authority/tribunal of your state. Get a lawyer before filing.
