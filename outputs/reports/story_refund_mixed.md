### 2. Where you stand

- If you paid for goods or a service and got a defective product, deficient service or an unfair trade practice (for example a promised refund that never came), you can complain as a 'consumer' under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019. The Commission can order a refund, compensation and costs.

- A complaint can be filed before the District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (Section 35 of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019) and electronically through e-Jagriti, the national platform that integrated e-Daakhil and other legacy systems in 2025. You may first raise it with the National Consumer Helpline (1915 or 1800-11-4000), a free pre-litigation grievance route. The filing fee is nil when the value paid as consideration is up to Rs 5 lakh.

- Which Commission has pecuniary jurisdiction depends on the value of the goods or services paid as consideration: District Commission up to Rs 50 lakh; State Commission above Rs 50 lakh up to Rs 2 crore; National Commission above Rs 2 crore. This is not necessarily the amount of refund or compensation sought. Confirm the current limits before filing.

- A legal notice is not mandatory before a consumer complaint, but a written complaint to the seller or company with a reasonable deadline strengthens your case and is useful evidence.

**Time limit:** about 2 years applies (see sources). You appear to be inside it, but don't wait.
