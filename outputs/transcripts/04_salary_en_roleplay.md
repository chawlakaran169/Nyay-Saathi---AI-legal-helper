### 2. Where you stand

- An employer must pay earned wages on time and cannot make unauthorised deductions. Since 21 November 2025 the Code on Wages, 2019 replaced the Payment of Wages Act, 1936. Section 17 of the Code on Wages, 2019 generally requires monthly wages before the end of the 7th day of the next month; after removal, dismissal, retrenchment or resignation, wages are generally due within two working days. The appropriate Government may prescribe a different time limit, and transition/state rules can matter; confirm the rule for your case with the state Labour Department.

- For certain money due to a worker under a settlement, award or specified chapters of the Industrial Relations Code, 2020, Section 59 provides an application to the appropriate Government for recovery, ordinarily within one year (a late application may be accepted for sufficient cause). This is not a general recovery route for every unpaid-salary claim. Ask your state Labour Department or a lawyer which route and deadline cover your case.

**Time limit:** labour forums can have short limits; act quickly.

### 3. Your options, in order

1. Send a written demand to HR/employer for pending salary and full-and-final settlement.

2. Complain to your state Labour Commissioner / Labour Department.

3. If you are a 'worker', you may be able to apply to recover the money due from the employer under the Industrial Relations Code; ask the Labour Department, a lawyer or free legal aid.

### 4. What to do this week

- Put all your documents and screenshots in one folder and back them up.

- Write a one-page timeline: date, what happened, who said what.

- Do not agree to anything verbally; keep communication in writing.

- Keep every document: agreement, receipts, bank or UPI records, chats, emails, photos. For electronic records (WhatsApp, emails, screenshots), a court may require the prescribed certificate under Section 63 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023. Proceedings pending immediately before that law came into force on 1 July 2024 remain governed by Section 65B of the Indian Evidence Act, 1872. Keep the original phone and a backup.

- Free legal aid is available under the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 to eligible people (for example women, children, SC/ST members and people below the income limit set by the state). Call NALSA on 15100 or visit your District Legal Services Authority.
